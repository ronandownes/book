# Irish School Maths — Editorial and technology standard

This is an **author-facing** design and maintenance guide, not student-facing textbook material.

## A single authoring system

- **PreTeXt XML** is the source of truth for structure, prose, mathematics, learning intentions, examples, exercises, answers, glossary, internal references and printable material.
- **MathJax** renders PreTeXt mathematics on the web; **LaTeX** typesets PDF output. Do not invent separate inline HTML maths.
- **TikZ and SVG** are used for static mathematical illustrations and can be reused in printable output.
- **Small HTML/JavaScript interactives** are used only when interaction materially contributes to understanding. Store their source in the Maths Apps repository; the book includes a tested interactive link/embedding where helpful.
- **PreTeXt native exercises and feedback** come first for assessment; only consider WeBWorK/Runestone for capabilities native exercises cannot provide. Do not add platforms without a specific requirement and a migration plan.
- **Git/GitHub** track source, **GitHub Actions** build, **GitHub Pages** publishes. Avoid manual editing of compiled HTML.
- **CSS** can colour programme navigation and improve readability but must not alter official source wording, meaning, semantic HTML or mathematical notation.

## Structural hierarchy

Book → programme **Part** → mathematical topic **Chapter** → concept or activity **Section** → **Subsection** only where it improves organisation.

Top-level parts (each programme gets one recognisable colour):
1. Junior Cycle — green
2. Transition Year — purple
3. Leaving Certificate — blue
4. Foundation Level — amber
5. Leaving Certificate Applied — teal
6. Appendices — slate

Do **not** repeat 'JC', 'LC', 'TY' in chapter names when the parent part already states the programme. It is acceptable and necessary to distinguish identical strand names in the cross-programme Appendices part.

Each new chapter and section must get a permanent descriptive `xml:id`. Do not number the ID: numbering is generated by PreTeXt and will change.

## Consistent chapter design

A chapter normally contains:
1. A concise motivating introduction showing why the mathematics matters.
2. Learning objectives / intended mathematical understanding.
3. Sections that build from fundamental concept to representations and principles.
4. Several methods only when they are genuinely useful, with their mathematical equivalence and strengths compared.
5. Worked examples, guided practice and independent exercises, with feedback or answers where appropriate.
6. A short synthesis or review, followed by an assessment block only when justified.

Avoid empty heading placeholders. Teaching sequence and assessment complexity differ by topic.

## Consistent section design

A section normally has a specific title and one primary mathematical idea. Use:
- **Concept:** meaning, definitions, prerequisites and relationships.
- **Representation:** diagrams, algebra, tables, words, graphs where pedagogically useful.
- **Principle:** what makes the mathematics true.
- **Method/procedure:** a particular reliable application of the principle; distinguish methods from representations.
- **Examples:** accurate, clearly staged, with unit consistency and sign checks.
- **Practice:** a prediction, checkpoint, exercise or worked counterexample.
- **Reflection:** explain, justify, compare methods or interpret the answer.

These are editorial checks, not student-visible labels that must appear on every page.

## Learning outcomes and assessment

- Maintain the **official NCCA curriculum** separately from the book's instructional interpretation; preserve the exact wording when the published source is reproduced, and retain attribution.
- Give each official outcome a stable identifier and map it to one or more section `xml:id` values.
- Use PreTeXt `<exercise>`, `<activity>`, `<investigation>`, `<example>`, `<solution>`, `<answer>` and `<hint>` rather than ad hoc quizzes.
- A question may have multiple representations but should assess a clear learning objective.
- Prefer informative feedback that diagnoses understanding, not merely right/wrong.
- Printable worksheets use PreTeXt worksheet components, not screenshots of web pages.

## Glossary and indexing

- Keep one authoritative mathematical glossary in `source/shared-glossary.ptx`, included in the Appendices part.
- Definitions use mathematically precise, learner-friendly language, followed by notation or examples only where useful.
- New concepts receive stable glossary IDs (`glossary-...`). Use contextual cross-references and `<idx>` tags at first substantive occurrences.
- Avoid automatically linking every instance of a word. Prefer first-use contextual links, key explanatory sections, and the search/index.
- Differentiate concept, principle, method, representation, heuristic, equation and expression consistently.

## Accessibility and quality gates

Use clear paragraph lengths; 12-point or larger reference text; comfortable line height; semantic headings; high contrast; alt/short descriptions; keyboard-friendly interaction; avoid meaning conveyed by colour alone. Keep diagrams mathematically accurate.

Before publishing, run:
1. XML and PreTeXt schema checks;
2. curriculum transcription completeness and source comparison;
3. HTML build;
4. navigation, glossary and link checks;
5. quick visual inspection in desktop and mobile dimensions.

A successful GitHub commit is **not** a successful deployment. Confirm the GitHub Actions deployment result.
