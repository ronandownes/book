What do the number families mean?

Square-free: no prime occurs twice, e.g. 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. A square-free semiprime has two distinct primes; a sphenic number has three.

Prime powers allowed: any prime may repeat, e.g. 12 = 2² × 3.

Powerful: every prime that occurs has exponent at least two, e.g. 72 = 2³ × 3².

In the first two modes, the number of prime factors counts repeats. In powerful mode, the selector counts distinct primes, to keep the examples manageable.

For HCF, choose the smallest exponent in each column (including zero). For LCM, choose the largest exponent. Multiply the resulting prime powers.
